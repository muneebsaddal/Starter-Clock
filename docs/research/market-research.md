**Status:** Partial; T002 blocked on customer-review and web-competitor evidence

**Last updated:** 2026-06-21

**Access date for all sources:** 2026-06-21

This document is the canonical owner of competitor, customer, pricing, and
positioning evidence. Facts, vendor claims, customer signals, and lead-agent
inferences are deliberately separated.

## Research Questions and Method

T002 asks whether home bakers have a feed-to-peak planning problem, whether
existing products leave a useful gap, who experiences it, and whether one free
starter plus a lifetime purchase near USD 19.99 is plausible.

Products are compared on feeding speed, peak/readiness estimation, observed
peak learning, reminders, history/photos, offline operation, platform coverage,
and price. This pass queried Apple's public US Search and Lookup APIs and the
US Google Play search surface. Store descriptions are vendor claims, not
independent proof. Apple rating counts are aggregate customer signals; written
review themes remain unverified because the review-feed request was stopped by
the research environment's usage limit.

## Customer Evidence

### Verified aggregate signals

- Loaflo and Rise each have hundreds of US Apple ratings and high averages,
  showing that adjacent sourdough/bread assistance attracts real use and can be
  well received.
- Dedicated newer trackers have much smaller rating samples: 21 for Sourdough
  Starter Tracker and 20 for Yeasta. These counts do not establish market size
  or recurring pain points.
- BreadMe's 40 ratings at a USD 2.99 paid-download price show some willingness
  to pay for a bread-planning utility, but do not validate a USD 19.99 price.

### Missing written-review evidence

No recurring customer-review theme is asserted yet. The task requires a sample
of attributable written reviews across material iOS and Android competitors,
including negative and mixed reviews. Aggregate ratings cannot reveal whether
users struggle with logging friction, reminder reliability, opaque estimates,
missing history, subscription fatigue, or anything else.

## Hypothesis Assessment

| Core hypothesis | Current assessment | Confidence and reason |
|---|---|---|
| Bakers need help translating a feeding into expected peak timing. | **Supported only by vendor behavior, not yet customer-validated.** Multiple vendors advertise readiness, rise forecasting, starter schedules, or “what's next.” | Low: competitor investment indicates a perceived job, but written customer evidence is missing. |
| Existing tools leave a gap for fast logging plus a transparent, personal peak window. | **Plausible but narrowed.** Close substitutes already cover fast logs, ratios, temperature, reminders, offline use, history, photos, and readiness guidance. Few reviewed listings explicitly claim a transparent window improved by the user's observed peaks. | Medium-low: the apparent gap comes from feature claims, not hands-on or review evidence. |
| Newer and regular home bakers are the best initial users. | **Plausible, not validated.** Competitor copy repeatedly targets beginners and home bakers; scheduling products also target experienced bakers. | Low: these are vendor-selected audiences. |
| One free starter and a lifetime Pro unlock near USD 19.99 can work. | **Not validated.** The market shows free, USD 2.99, USD 5.99, subscriptions, tips, and unspecified one-time/lifetime unlocks. | Low: exact current in-app prices and willingness-to-pay evidence are missing. |

## Decision-Ready Direction, Pending Missing Evidence

The defensible product wedge is narrower than “a sourdough tracker.” Competing
head-on on logging, reminders, calculators, recipes, AI advice, or generic
history would enter a crowded feature set. The candidate wedge is:

> After each feeding, show a calm, explainable peak window, let the baker record
> what actually happened, and use that starter's history to make later windows
> more useful.

Keep the chartered MVP boundary for now, but treat progress photos as secondary
and keep recipes, AI advice, social features, and cloud accounts out. T003
should make peak-window transparency, missing-input behavior, observed-peak
capture, reminder reliability, and sub-15-second logging the core acceptance
criteria.

Do not lock USD 19.99 for release. Test at least a lower one-time utility anchor
and the chartered USD 19.99 anchor after the core prediction experience can be
demonstrated. Do not add subscriptions merely because competitors use them.
