# Starter Clock Market Research

**Status:** Complete for T002

**Last updated:** 2026-06-21

**Access date for all sources:** 2026-06-21

This document is the canonical owner of competitor, customer, pricing, and
positioning evidence. Facts, vendor claims, customer opinions, and lead-agent
inferences are deliberately separated.

## Research Questions and Method

T002 asks whether home bakers have a feed-to-peak planning problem, whether
existing products leave a useful gap, who experiences it, and whether one free
starter plus a lifetime purchase near USD 19.99 is plausible.

Products were compared on feeding speed, peak/readiness estimation,
observed-peak learning, reminders, history/photos, offline operation, platform
coverage, and price. Evidence came from current US Apple and Google Play
listings, Apple public review feeds, visible Google Play reviews, and live web
tools. Store and website descriptions are vendor claims, not independent proof.

The written-review sample contains the first 43 available reviews from the
most-recent Apple feed pages for Sourdough Starter Tracker (6), Rise (25), and
BreadMe (12), plus all three reviews visible on the US Google Play listing for
Sourdough Starter Tracker: 46 reviews total. Reviews were manually coded;
themes may overlap. This is a directional convenience sample, not a
representative market survey. Loaflo's Apple feed returned no written entries
despite its aggregate rating count.

Sources: [Sourdough Starter Tracker Apple reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6755709217/sortby=mostrecent/json),
[Rise Apple reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1515369685/sortby=mostrecent/json),
[BreadMe Apple reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1522824861/sortby=mostrecent/json),
and the visible reviews on the [Sourdough Starter Tracker Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.quickseedlabs.sourdoughstarter&hl=en_US&gl=US).

| Coded theme | Reviews mentioning it | Directional interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Planning, tracking, or reminders reduce cognitive load | 19 | The strongest recurring job is remembering state and timing across long, interruptible processes. |
| Calculators, scaling, or dynamic timing create value | 10 | Bakers value having arithmetic and schedule adjustments handled together. |
| History, photos, notes, or iteration support learning | 9 | Repeatability and improvement matter, especially to regular bakers. |
| Beginner guidance or confidence | 8 | Newer bakers explicitly value reduced uncertainty and an approachable starting point. |
| Purchase described as worthwhile or purchase intent expressed | 6 positive, 1 negative | Some willingness to pay exists, but the sample cannot establish a price point. |
| Bug, ad, sync, usability, or missing-flexibility complaint/request | 15 | Reliability and low-friction editing are table stakes; several otherwise-positive reviews still request fixes. |

Representative attributable examples, paraphrased to avoid over-weighting
individual wording:

- Google Play reviewers for Sourdough Starter Tracker said reminders and the
  guide made starter care less daunting, particularly for distracted beginners.
- An Apple reviewer called its calculator and photo record worth paying for;
  another tied premium purchase intent to correct retroactive starter-age
  handling. Other reviews reported cross-device sync trouble, inappropriate ad
  content, and insufficient observation choices.
- Rise reviewers repeatedly valued step timing, reminders, calculators, and
  the ability to focus on baking instead of tracking. Negative or mixed reports
  included launch/loading failure, mid-process quitting, import failure,
  calculator doubts, missing small units/end-time scheduling, and no timer pause.
- BreadMe reviewers valued reminders and replacing scattered timers or paper
  notes, while others reported weak onboarding, unit friction, and missing
  recipe-linking or editing flexibility.

## Hypothesis Assessment

| Core hypothesis | Decision | Confidence and reason |
|---|---|---|
| Bakers need help translating feeding and fermentation state into usable timing. | **Supported.** | Moderate: recurring review themes directly support timing, reminder, calculation, and cognitive-load jobs; web and mobile vendors invest in readiness tools. The sample is directional rather than representative. |
| Existing tools leave a gap for fast logging plus a transparent, personal peak window. | **Supported only as a narrow combination.** | Moderate-low: close substitutes cover nearly every individual feature, and free web tools already estimate peak/readiness. The unclaimed combination is persistent mobile logging + explainable window + observed-peak learning + derived reminder. This gap is imitable and still needs prototype validation. |
| Newer and regular home bakers are the best initial users. | **Supported as two initial segments.** | Moderate-low: beginners value guidance/confidence; regular bakers value planning, calculators, history, and iteration. No evidence justifies professional bakery scope. |
| One free starter and a lifetime Pro unlock near USD 19.99 can work. | **Plausible experiment, not validated pricing.** | Low: close price anchors range from USD 9.99 premium to USD 19.99 once and USD 69.99 lifetime, with subscriptions and free utilities also present. Reviews show willingness to pay but not price elasticity. |

## MVP and Positioning Implications

The defensible position is not “another sourdough tracker” or merely “a peak
calculator.” It is:

> After each feeding, show a calm, explainable peak window, remind the baker at
> the useful moment, capture what actually happened, and make later windows more
> useful for that starter.

Recommended MVP boundary:

- Keep fast feeding logs, ratio/hydration math, estimated peak window, local
  reminder, editable history, observed-peak capture, offline operation, and one
  free starter at the center.
- Keep photos secondary: useful for history, but not part of the differentiating
  promise.
- Require transparent factors and a window rather than false precision; web
  competitors already warn users to observe the starter rather than trust a
  clock blindly.
- Keep recipes, AI advice, social features, inventory, sensors, and cloud
  accounts outside the MVP. Competitors are already broader; breadth does not
  strengthen the validated job.
- Treat reliability, retroactive editing, unit flexibility, and graceful
  reminder behavior as acceptance concerns because review complaints make them
  purchase-relevant.

Pricing recommendation: preserve the chartered USD 19.99 lifetime price as one
experiment, not a release decision. Test USD 9.99 and USD 19.99 one-time anchors
against the same demonstrated core experience. Define the free history limit
in T003, then validate comprehension and value before selecting a price. Current
evidence does not support adding a subscription.

## Evidence Limits and Follow-ups

- Store review samples are self-selected, platform-skewed, and small for the
  closest dedicated trackers.
- Vendor claims and listing availability were verified; product behavior was
  not tested hands-on.
- Search discovery surfaced several apparently new or low-authority web tools;
  only live, reachable calculators with clear inputs and outputs were compared.
- T004 prototype testing should verify that users understand the window and
  observed-peak loop. Pricing research should follow a demonstrable prototype.
- None of this evidence proves scientific prediction accuracy. T005 must define
  and test the deterministic model separately.
